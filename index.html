How I'd sell Hex in 2026

By Minjae Lee

Every B2B SaaS vendor wants to sell agents. But to sell more of them, we first need to understand the pain prospects commonly face: enterprise agents are getting harder to deploy and administer, because there are more of them and every team uses them differently, some as direct contributors and some not. So it matters to see what people ask agents, and to decide who gets to keep enriching their context.

01Trust is the moat

Across 200+ dbt platform customers, I kept hearing the same worry from data teams: they are not part of the agent narrative. Many business teams assume that with agents, they will no longer need a data team.

Data teams are still central to the success of a deal, so my job is to help them step into two new roles: context engineers and AI analytics platform administrators. That changes the pitch. Instead of telling prospects they can ship more and more agents with Hex, I would show them how to make the agents they build trustworthy. I would walk it in three steps:

  1. Raise data quality. Standardize data transformation, then add a semantic layer to govern metrics. Hex can author semantic models or sync them from dbt MetricFlow, Cube and Snowflake Semantic Views.
  2. Make the agent's knowledge base smarter. Context Studio shows what agents are asked, surfaces where context is missing, and suggests fixes.
  3. Make the workspace trustworthy and governable. Certified statuses and endorsements tell agents which tables and projects to prefer, and exclusions keep them away from the rest. Business users give feedback in Threads, and that feedback feeds back into context.

Governance, and the automation around it, has been top of mind for me for a while. Customers tell me AI has lowered the barrier to entry so far that the business asks: if we can build agents ourselves, why do we need a data team? At dbt, that pushed me to advocate for data mesh ownership and for product ideas like verified queries, verified metadata, and a review bot that approves or flags pull requests.

02Micro product tours

I have covered commercial, enterprise and majors accounts. In mid-market, time with each account is short, so an end-to-end platform demo works against you. I would break Hex into short product tours, each built around one use case, and show the one that resonates with the person in front of me.

  1. Find one person and one use case. Discovery first: what are they trying to answer, and who do they report it to?
  2. Show one workflow really well. A two to three minute product tour of one workflow.
  3. Turn a fan into a referral. When the content lands, that person vouches for us and introduces us to the rest of the org.

A real scenario

A customer inherited a dbt platform contract through an acquisition. They had run dbt Core with Dagster for orchestration, and their BI had moved from Tableau to Mode to Hex. Their question was fair: why administer another tool? Telemetry showed the new admins had no dbt platform seat, and they were building Hex agents with no semantic model behind them.

Confirm the orchestrator name (Dagster). Keep the account anonymous.

How I would tell the story

  1. Start from their plan. You are rolling out Hex agents. Where will your governed metrics live, and how will every agent get the same definition?
  2. Connect the layers they already have. Your logic already lives in dbt. Author the metadata and metrics there too. Hex reads dbt descriptions, tests and freshness, and syncs MetricFlow metrics into Hex semantic models. On Databricks, persist docs carries the same metadata into Unity Catalog.
  3. Land the outcome. One version-controlled definition, reused by Hex agents today and any other agent tomorrow. Both renewals get stronger.

04Brand is a moat

At the end of the day, this is a sales role. People remember the personality behind a technical discovery or a demo. I do not want to be just the demo.

There is a reason B2B companies with open source roots invest in user groups, Slack and Discord communities, in-person events, and keynotes that feel like a show. In a crowded and anxious market, where every vendor is pitching agents, people buy from people they remember.

Hex gets this. At every conference I attend, the Hex activation is on my top two to visit, for the experience and for the merch. That is not fluff. It is where trust starts, long before a demo.

How I would show up

  1. Low ego, high curiosity. Conversations that feel like a FaceTime with a friend.
  2. Personality in the demo. Customers link the person they talked to with the product and the brand.
  3. Genuinely bought in. I already use and follow Hex. Being proud of what I sell makes me better at selling it, and more fun to work with.
