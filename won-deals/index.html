Business moment: Protecting margins in a volatile economy

Trusted metrics, ready for AI agents

The problem
In a volatile U.S. economy, even household-name restaurant brands fight for every point of margin. Leadership needed an accurate read on customer loyalty and retention, seamless restaurant and supply chain operations, and less manual work, all while expanding outside the U.S.
How I sold it
I tied their AI ambition to data trust. Agents can only automate operations if loyalty, retention and supply chain metrics mean the same thing in every team and every market. One governed definition of each metric became the foundation for their agentic AI plans.
The outcome
A trusted metric foundation for agentic AI, from customer loyalty to the supply chain, built to scale into new markets.

What it shows: Connecting data work to what the executive team actually cares about, and to AI.

Business moment: Preparing for an exit

A renewal that gave the data team the upper hand

The problem
The business was being acquired. In a merger, the buyer often decides whose tools and whose teams stay. The data team needed to show their stack was the one worth keeping.
How I sold it
I framed the renewal around their position, not ours. Their architecture was already mature, scalable and efficient, built on current best practices. Renewing kept that foundation in place and gave the team proof they were running the cutting edge.
The outcome
Renewal closed. The data team went into the merger with a stack the acquirer could not easily replace, and a real say in what comes next. A win for both sides.

What it shows: Selling to the customer’s position, not just the product.

Business moment: Modernizing a legacy stack

Legacy ETL retired, multi-year signed

The problem
A legacy ETL tool was burning through an expensive contract, and the team needed to modernize without slowing the business down.
How I sold it
I built the business case to cut that spend and get started right away. I paired the platform with a professional services engagement so the team could move fast, and I built the relationship in person at Coalesce (now dbt Summit).
The outcome
  • Legacy ETL tool deprecated quickly
  • Multi-year agreement signed, based on the long-term potential they saw
  • Professional services engagement extended

What it shows: Moving across go-to-market swim lanes: platform, professional services and in-person events.
